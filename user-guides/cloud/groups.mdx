Open **Cloud > Groups** to review synchronized Microsoft 365 and Google Workspace groups available to your account. Company users see accessible groups that their MSP has kept visible to clients. MSP readers can also see groups hidden from clients.

**Audience:** Company users with Microsoft 365 read access and MSP staff. The same read permission covers Google groups. Your MSP manages access and provider account changes.

## Open a group

1. Open **Cloud**, then select **Groups**.
2. Check the company selector in the top bar. Select the company you are reviewing if your account can choose companies.
3. Find the group using the **Group** or **Email** column filter. Check Company and Type to distinguish groups with similar names.
4. Click the group row to open its member dialog.
5. Check the group name, company and provider badge before reading the list. Close the dialog with its close control or Escape when finished.

A group appearing here does not make you a member or grant access to its resources. To request a membership change, [open a ticket through the Home AI Assistant](/user-guides/ticketing/open-a-ticket) with the company, group, person and requested change.

## Understand the group list

| Column | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Group** | Synchronized name, with description beneath it when present. |
| **Email** | Recorded group email, or a dash. A security group can have no email. |
| **Provider / Company** | Source and mapped company. Provider is omitted for a single-provider scope; Company is omitted when the top bar selects one company. |
| **Type** | Distribution, Security, Microsoft 365, Mail-enabled security, Google group, or Other, depending on synchronized records. |
| **Members** | Recorded count; **Not available** means no count was supplied. This is different from zero. |
| **Aliases** | Additional recorded addresses. Hidden by default; Microsoft rows do not supply aliases in this view. **None** is not proof of no aliases in the provider. |
| **Last synced** | Synchronization date for the group record, hidden by default. It does not separately report when every member was last checked. |

Use **Columns**, select the extra fields, then **Save Changes**. **Cancel** or closing the panel discards unsaved choices; **Reset** also needs Save Changes to apply. Scope-dependent columns cannot be forced into view. MSP staff with write access also see [Client visible controls](/user-guides/cloud/manage-group-visibility).

## Read the member dialog

The dialog lists **Name**, **Email**, **Type**, **Role**, and **Status** for the synchronized member records. A missing name can fall back to email; missing values use a dash or **Not available**.

Microsoft member rows show **Not available** for Role and Status in this view. This does not mean the member has no role or that the account is disabled. Google rows can supply member role and status, but these are synchronized group-member attributes rather than a complete account-security review.

Use **Previous** and **Next** to read more members; the dialog loads 100 per page. Opening another group starts its member list at page 1. The count beside the group name comes from the group record; the pager counts synchronized member rows. They can differ when source counts and member synchronization do not agree.

A nested group can appear as a member record. There is no nested-group expansion or recursive effective-membership view in this dialog. There is also no member search, CSV export, or add/remove-member control here.

## Missing or stale membership

**No members were synchronized for this group** means this view has no member rows to show. The group may be empty or its members may not have synchronized. A missing group can also reflect filters, company access, mapping, or client visibility. Ask your MSP to check the provider and synchronization before treating either case as a completed access review or offboarding result.

Company users should request corrections from their MSP. There is no Sync now action on Cloud Groups. Provider connection and synchronization management are separate MSP workflows.

## FAQs

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can I add or remove a group member here?">
    No. The dialog reads synchronized membership. Ask your MSP through a support ticket to make the provider change.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does opening a group give me access to its files or email?">
    No. Viewing synchronized information does not change group membership or provider permissions.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does an empty dialog prove the group has no members?">
    No. It can mean membership has not synchronized. Confirm the provider state with your MSP, especially during an access review or offboarding.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why does the member count differ from the dialog list?">
    The heading uses the group record count; the list uses synchronized member records. Synchronization or source-data differences can make them disagree. Ask your MSP to investigate.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Why do Microsoft members show Not available for Role and Status?">
    Those fields are not supplied for Microsoft rows in this view. It does not establish account status or absence of a role.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can I expand a nested group to see all effective members?">
    No. Nested groups can appear as member records, but the dialog does not recursively expand them. Your MSP can verify effective membership in the provider.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does Aliases = None prove there are no other group addresses?">
    No. Microsoft aliases are not supplied in this view. Google aliases reflect synchronized data. Ask your MSP to verify the provider if aliases matter.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Does Last synced prove every member is current?">
    No. This date belongs to the group record. It is not a separate freshness guarantee for every synchronized membership entry.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
